# ADR-0010: Re-fetch a TMDB season on a miss, not on a timer

Episode titles are fetched a season at a time rather than an episode at a time, so marking several episodes off one season costs one request instead of one per episode. The complication is that TMDB publishes a season's episodes as they air: a season mid-run lists only what has been announced so far — Grey's Anatomy S23 listed 1 episode against an eventual ~18 when this was checked. The Watch Next page lists a season whenever the *viewer* has episodes outstanding on it, which says nothing about whether the season has finished airing. A show started after its run ended has a complete map that will never change; a season still airing has one that grows underneath it. Only the second kind goes stale, and the page carries enough of them that a saved map cannot be trusted indefinitely.

Caching the map for the life of the application process was tried first and is a regression against the per-episode code it replaced: a newly aired episode is absent from the stale map, answers `None`, and never triggers a re-fetch, on a container that runs for months. A six-hour expiry was tried next. It bounds the bug without fixing it, and is the wrong instrument in both directions — a newly aired episode has no title for up to six hours, while a finished season is re-downloaded every six hours forever for an answer that cannot change.

What is used instead: a settled answer is never asked about again, and a *miss* is what forces the re-fetch. A miss is exact evidence that the saved map cannot answer, where a timer is a guess. A season whose titles have all resolved therefore costs zero requests however often the page is rendered, and a newly aired episode resolves on the next render. The hold-off (`_REFETCH_FLOOR`) is keyed per episode rather than per season, because a per-season timestamp cannot distinguish "not asked about yet" from "just asked about" and so blocks the very first miss — the case the whole design exists to serve. Transient failures record nothing, so a timeout or a 429 retries on the next render rather than being held off.

The re-fetch pulls the whole season rather than the one missing episode, which is counter-intuitive and was measured before being chosen. Both requests take about 35ms — TMDB's own latency dominates and the extra ~20KB does not register — so the currency is request count, not bytes. They tie in every case except one: returning to a season with ten episodes backed up, the whole-season fetch fills the entire backlog in one request, while fetching one episode at a time costs ten requests, each blocking a mark-watched tap.
